# Breure Media — Content DNA

> Internal editorial operating model. This document defines **what Breure Media
> publishes, why, in which order, and under which constraints**. It is the
> reference for future AI agents, Cursor sessions, editors, and the owner.
>
> Visual craft, tone of voice, and UI still follow [`design-dna.md`](design-dna.md).
> This file governs content strategy, not pixels.

**Audience:** AI agents, Cursor sessions, editors, and the owner.

**How to use it:** before proposing or creating a page, product list, cluster,
or affiliate placement, test the idea against this document. If the answers
are weak, do not publish.

## 1. Identity

Breure Media is a **content, publishing, and affiliate-oriented reading
platform**.

It is:

- an independent Dutch imprint for literary thrillers and original stories
- a place to discover worthwhile books and understand which book fits which reader
- a source of editorial buying guidance for books and e-readers
- a site that grows through **useful topic clusters**, not disconnected thin pages

It is not:

- a product dump or marketplace clone
- a doorway-page farm
- a catalog of affiliate buttons with filler text
- a news site, deal blog, or “top 10 every week” machine

### Current core themes

- books and reading
- own publications and stories
- books connected to locations
- e-readers and digital reading
- editorial buying guidance

### Current strong content pillars

These already exist and should be deepened first:

| Pillar | Live examples (not exhaustive) |
|---|---|
| Own publications | `/boeken`, title pages such as *Schaduwen over Domburg* |
| Books about Rotterdam | `/boeken-over-rotterdam` |
| Books about Zeeland | `/boeken-over-zeeland` |
| E-readers and digital reading | `/e-readers`, `/waterdichte-e-reader`, `/lengte-van-ebook` |
| Editorial hub | `/blog` |

Future location clusters (for example Brussels) may be added **only** when
there is enough editorial relevance, content depth, and strategic fit. A
setting in an own title is not by itself enough to justify a cluster.

The site should grow through **depth inside validated themes**, not by
publishing isolated pages around whatever keyword happens to appear.

## 2. Editorial mission

Breure Media should help visitors:

- discover worthwhile books
- choose between relevant books
- understand which book fits which reader
- discover books around a city, region, theme, or interest
- understand e-readers and digital reading
- compare products before buying
- make better purchase decisions

The site must not merely aggregate products. **Every page must add meaningful
editorial value.**

Value looks like:

- curation
- comparison
- context
- explanation
- selection criteria
- reader profiles
- honest pros and cons
- category structure
- original summaries
- purchase guidance
- internal recommendations

If a draft could be replaced by a retailer product listing without loss, it
is not ready.

---

## 3. Core principle: topic clusters

Publish in **topic clusters**, not isolated articles.

A cluster has:

1. **One pillar page** — the broad, high-value overview
2. **Supporting pages** — each answering a distinct reader question
3. **Logical internal links** — pillar ↔ supporting pages, plus links between
   closely related supporting pages

### Example: Rotterdam books cluster

Possible structure (examples, **not a mandatory backlog**):

- **Pillar:** Books about Rotterdam
- Rotterdam history books
- Rotterdam architecture books
- Rotterdam harbour books
- Feyenoord books
- Rotterdam photography books
- Rotterdam crime / thrillers
- Rotterdam children’s books
- Rotterdam walking guides
- Rotterdam travel / city guides
- books every Rotterdammer should read
- books for visitors to Rotterdam

Create a supporting page **only** when there is enough real content and a
clear reason for the page to exist. A thin heading with three affiliate
buttons is not a cluster page.

The pillar should help users discover the supporting pages. Supporting pages
should link back to the pillar and to closely related pages.

Apply the same model later to Zeeland, Brussels, e-readers, and other
**validated** themes.

### Cluster rules

- Do not invent a cluster because a keyword exists.
- Do not create near-duplicate pages that cannibalize each other.
- Prefer expanding a strong existing page over starting a weak new one.
- Cross-link related clusters where it genuinely helps the reader
  (Rotterdam ↔ Zeeland is a current example).

## 4. Data-informed content prioritization

Do not select books or topics on intuition alone.

Use a **three-signal framework**. All three matter. No single signal is
enough to justify publication.

### Signal 1 — Search demand

Ask whether people actively look for the topic.

Possible sources:

- Google Search Console
- Google Trends
- keyword research tools
- Ahrefs, Semrush, Mangools, LowFruits
- other credible search-demand tools

**Rules:**

- Volume estimates are **directional**, not absolute truth.
- A low-volume keyword may still be valuable if it has strong intent or
  supports a strategic cluster.
- Do not chase every low-volume variant.

### Signal 2 — Marketplace demand

Ask whether the book or product appears to have proven commercial demand.

Possible sources:

- bol.com marketplace observations
- MarktMentor or similar tools
- bestseller rankings
- marketplace category rankings
- visible popularity signals
- sales-estimation tools

**Rules:**

- MarktMentor and similar tools provide **estimated** marketplace demand, not
  official bol.com sales figures.
- Never present estimates internally or publicly as confirmed sales data
  unless the underlying source actually provides verified figures.
- Marketplace demand is a **prioritization signal**. It is not sufficient by
  itself to justify publication.

### Signal 3 — Editorial value

Ask:

- Is this genuinely relevant to Breure Media?
- Can we add original value?
- Does it belong in an existing or planned cluster?
- Can we explain why someone should choose it?
- Does the topic help the reader make a decision?
- Can Breure Media produce something better than a simple product listing?
- Does it strengthen topical authority?

Editorial value is essential. A topic with strong sales but no editorial fit
should **not** automatically be published.

## 5. Content opportunity scoring

When prioritizing books, products, or articles, evaluate:

1. Search demand
2. Marketplace demand
3. Editorial value
4. Commercial intent
5. Cluster fit
6. Competition difficulty
7. Ability to produce genuinely better content
8. Internal-link potential
9. Long-term relevance

Do not require exact mathematical scoring unless it becomes useful.

A simple qualitative scale is enough:

- **High**
- **Medium**
- **Low**

Future agents may create a weighted model if sufficient data exists. The
purpose is **prioritization, not false precision**.

A High/High/Low pattern (strong demand, weak editorial fit) is usually a
**no**. A Medium/Medium/High pattern inside a live cluster is often a
**yes**.

## 6. Book selection principles

When selecting books for a page, prefer titles with one or more of:

- proven reader interest
- strong marketplace demand
- relevant search demand
- authoritative or distinctive subject matter
- strong fit with the page
- usefulness for comparison
- strong local or theme relevance
- a clear reader need they help answer

**Do not choose only bestsellers.**

A strong editorial page may mix:

- popular books
- niche specialist titles
- classics
- new releases
- local-interest books
- practical guides
- visual / photography books
- fiction
- non-fiction

The mix should serve the reader of that page, not a commercial quota.

Own titles may appear in a cluster when they genuinely belong there. Do not
force an own title onto a page where it is a weak fit.

## 7. Affiliate philosophy

Affiliate monetization **follows the content**. It does not control it.

Preferred order:

1. Identify a useful topic
2. Create high-value editorial content
3. Recommend relevant products or books
4. Add affiliate opportunities where appropriate

Do not create thin pages whose sole purpose is to contain affiliate buttons.

If a useful book is available on bol.com but Breure Media does not yet have
a bol.com affiliate relationship, it is acceptable to:

- include the book
- write original editorial text
- use a normal non-affiliate link
- convert the link later if approved

Revenue must never be prioritized above content quality.

Affiliate disclosure must be transparent wherever affiliate links appear.
Relationships must not determine editorial opinion. That is already the
public position on `/affiliate` and must remain true in practice.

## 8. Retailer strategy

Breure Media may use multiple retailers.

Examples:

- Amazon
- bol.com
- other relevant book or e-reader retailers later

Do not force one retailer on every product.

Where appropriate, give the user a choice of retailer.

Retailer selection may consider:

- availability
- language
- local market relevance
- consumer trust
- price visibility
- device ecosystem
- affiliate availability

Practical defaults, not dogma:

- For **Dutch books and Dutch consumers**, bol.com may sometimes offer
  stronger product availability than Amazon.
- For **Kindle-related products**, Amazon may naturally be more relevant.

Do not make unsupported conversion claims (“this retailer converts better”)
without evidence.

The e-reader cluster should focus on **decision support**.

Potential topics (examples, not a mandatory backlog):

- best e-readers
- waterproof e-readers
- Kobo vs Kindle
- e-reader for seniors
- e-reader for travel
- e-reader for reading in bed
- e-reader with colour screen
- how to choose an e-reader
- individual product reviews
- comparisons
- beginner guides
- e-reader terminology

**Each page must answer a distinct user question.** Avoid many near-duplicate

Live supporting pages already include waterproof e-readers and e-book length.
Deepen those and fill genuine gaps before inventing overlapping variants.

E-readers are a **current core pillar**. Older tactical notes that treated
this cluster as low priority are superseded by this document.

## 10. Search intent

Before creating a page, identify its **primary intent**. Content structure
must match that intent.

| Intent | Typical question | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| Informational | What is an e-reader? | Explanation, definitions, context |
| Comparison | Kobo vs Kindle | Criteria, differences, who-it-is-for |
| Commercial investigation | Best waterproof e-reader | Selection criteria, shortlist, trade-offs |
| Transactional support | Where to buy this title | Clear retailer choice after editorial context |
| Local / topic discovery | Books about Rotterdam | Curated discovery, reader profiles, next reads |

Do not force transactional language into purely informational pages.

Examples:

- “Books about Rotterdam” → curated discovery / commercial investigation
- “Kobo vs Kindle” → comparison
- “What is an e-reader?” → informational
- “Best waterproof e-reader” → commercial investigation

## 11. Content quality standards

Every important article should aim to contain:

- a clear introduction
- a reader-focused structure
- original editorial text
- useful selection criteria
- clear headings
- internal links
- relevant product or book recommendations where appropriate
- transparent affiliate disclosure where relevant
- no copied retailer descriptions
- no filler

Avoid:

- generic AI prose
- repeated paragraphs
- keyword stuffing
- exaggerated claims
- fake personal experience
- copied marketplace text
- thin “top 10” articles with no analysis
- publishing pages purely to hit a keyword count

Voice still follows `design-dna.md`: calm, literary, never hysterical, no
clickbait, no fake urgency. Buying guidance may be practical without becoming
a deal blog.

## 12. Originality and AI assistance

AI may assist with:

- research
- structuring
- comparison
- outlining
- drafting
- data processing
- categorization
- internal-link suggestions

Final editorial content must be useful and factually checked.

**Do not pretend the author personally tested a product unless that actually
happened.**

When firsthand testing did not occur, use language such as:

- “Based on specifications…”
- “Suitable for readers who…”
- “This model stands out because…”

Do not invent anecdotes, birthday-party recommendations, or “I used this for
months” claims without a real basis.

As Breure Media grows, maintain structured data for books and products where
practical.

Possible fields:

- title
- author
- ISBN
- category
- topic cluster
- publication year
- retailer availability
- Amazon URL
- bol.com URL
- affiliate status
- search-demand signal
- marketplace-demand signal
- editorial-value score
- commercial-intent score
- notes

Do not build a complex database merely for architecture’s sake.

Current structured sources (`data/books.ts`, `data/affiliate.ts`,
`data/content-pages.ts`, `data/blog.ts`) should be extended when that
materially improves scalability — for example when the same title must appear
in multiple clusters without duplicated, drifting copy.

## 14. Update strategy

Pages should evolve over time.

Typical maintenance:

- add new books
- remove unavailable books
- update retailer links
- improve descriptions
- add internal links
- refresh e-reader specifications
- update “best” pages
- add new supporting cluster pages

A page is not finished forever once published.

Prefer **incremental improvement** over constant complete rewrites. Do not
rewrite a ranking page from scratch unless the current page is substantively
wrong or thin.

---

## 15. Prioritization rule

When time is limited, work in this order:

1. Existing pages already receiving impressions
2. Strong topic clusters
3. High-intent opportunities
4. Topics with both search and marketplace demand
5. Improvements to pages close to generating clicks or conversions
6. New speculative topics

This rule exists to prevent spreading effort across too many weak new URLs.

Current implication: deepen Rotterdam, Zeeland, e-readers, and own-title
pages before opening a new location cluster.

## 16. SEO philosophy

SEO should support the reader experience.

**Do:**

- clear topic structure
- useful titles
- strong internal links
- descriptive headings
- good metadata
- indexable high-quality pages
- sensible canonicals
- structured data where appropriate

**Do not:**

- produce doorway pages
- publish hundreds of thin variants
- over-optimize keyword density
- rewrite pages unnecessarily
- chase every low-volume keyword
- create fake reviews

Topical authority should come from genuine depth.

Search Console data is a prioritization input. Early, low-impression data is
statistically weak; do not churn metadata on the basis of a handful of
queries.

---

## 17. Conversion philosophy

Help the reader before asking for the click.

Good sequence:

**Need → explanation → selection guidance → recommendation → retailer choice → purchase link**

Buttons should be clear. Examples:

- Bekijk bij Amazon
- Bekijk bij bol
- Bekijk actuele prijs

Do not use deceptive urgency, countdown timers, fake scarcity, or
“bestseller” labels unless factually proven.

The primary button on a page should still feel like editorial design, not
marketplace chrome. See `design-dna.md` for visual constraints.

## 18. Bol.com approval context

Breure Media previously applied to the bol.com Affiliate Program.

The initial application was rejected because bol indicated that the site did
not yet contain enough relevant, value-added blog content to assess visitor
value and fit with the Affiliate Marketing Program.

**Current strategic objective:** deepen the site with substantive content
that helps visitors during purchase orientation.

**Do not optimize the entire website only for bol.com approval.**

The same improvements should also benefit:

- readers
- Google
- affiliate conversion (including existing Amazon relationships)
- long-term brand authority

Non-affiliate bol.com links are acceptable until approval exists. Do not
omit a useful Dutch title merely because the link cannot yet earn commission.

## 19. Long-term editorial direction

Breure Media should become a **trusted reading-oriented content platform**,
not a collection of affiliate pages.

The strongest long-term asset is:

**useful editorial depth + structured topic clusters + commercial relevance + consistent trust**

Affiliate revenue should be a consequence of that system, not the system
itself.

The literary-imprint identity remains the brand core. Cluster content should
feel like a serious publisher helping readers choose — not like a coupon
site that also sells a novel.

## 20. Decision rule for future AI agents

Before proposing or creating a new article, answer:

1. Which cluster does this belong to?
2. What reader question does it answer?
3. What evidence of demand exists?
4. Is there marketplace or commercial interest?
5. Can Breure Media add original value?
6. What existing pages should link to it?
7. What should it link to?
8. Is this better than improving an existing page?

If the answers are weak, **do not automatically create the page**.

Also refuse work that would:

- duplicate an existing URL
- invent firsthand testing
- copy retailer copy
- publish a thin affiliate shell
- open a new cluster without depth
- contradict `design-dna.md` on tone, craft, or visual quality
- treat estimates as verified sales figures

## What not to do

A short anti-pattern list for agents and editors:

- Do not publish disconnected thin pages to “cover keywords.”
- Do not build a cluster page without enough real content.
- Do not let affiliate availability decide which books deserve coverage.
- Do not present marketplace estimates as official sales data.
- Do not force one retailer on every product.
- Do not create near-duplicate comparison pages.
- Do not force buy-language into informational articles.
- Do not copy bol.com, Amazon, or publisher blurbs.
- Do not fake reviews or personal product tests.
- Do not rewrite ranking pages without a substantive reason.
- Do not chase bol.com approval at the expense of readers or Google.
- Do not treat `seo-actieplan.md` as current strategy.
- Do not turn the site into a generic webshop visually or editorially.

## Working checklist for a new page

Use this before a page is proposed, drafted, or shipped:

- [ ] Cluster is named, and this URL has a distinct job inside it
- [ ] Primary search intent is identified and the structure matches it
- [ ] Search demand, marketplace demand, and editorial value were considered
- [ ] This is better than expanding an existing page
- [ ] Internal links in and out are planned
- [ ] Copy is original, checked, and free of fake firsthand claims
- [ ] Recommendations serve the reader; affiliate links follow the content
- [ ] Retailer choice is justified, not forced
- [ ] Affiliate disclosure is present where relevant
- [ ] The page would still be worth reading if every buy button disappeared
- [ ] Tone and craft still pass `design-dna.md`
